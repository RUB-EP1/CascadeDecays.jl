General relations and signs

CascadeDecays (CD) follows helicity frames through each decay vertex. ThreeBodyDecays (TBD) factors out the orientation of a planar three-body event and aligns its spins with real Wigner small-$d$ matrices. They describe the same amplitude once the external spin bases, a continuous phase, and a discrete chain sign are matched.

This page gives the conversion first, explains where its angles and signs come from, then checks it component by component on sampled four-vectors. It follows Quantization reference frames in ThreeBodyDecays. For spin-zero daughters, see also the COMPASS example.

The relation to use

Write $k$ for the chain topology and $r=\mathtt{reference\_k}$ for the common quantization reference. Keep the cyclic daughter order:

IndexTopologyIsobar daughtersSpectator
1$(23)1$$(2,3)$1
2$(31)2$$(3,1)$2
3$(12)3$$(1,2)$3

For matched vertex conventions, LS couplings, particle-2 phases, normalization, and line shapes, the full complex tensors obey

\[\boxed{ A_{\mathrm{CD},\boldsymbol\lambda,m_0}^{k;r} =S(k,r)\,e^{i\lambda_r\gamma_r} A_{\mathrm{TBD},\boldsymbol\lambda,m_0}^{k;r}}\]

Here $\boldsymbol\lambda=(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3)$ are helicities in the reference frames, $m_0$ is the parent projection in the external frame, and $\gamma_r$ is the inner azimuth of the reference chain. Physical helicities appear in the exponential; Julia’s two_js and two_λ values must be divided by two. Both tensors use axes (λ1, λ2, λ3, m0).

The sign is particularly simple:

\[S(k,r)=\begin{cases} 1,& k=r,\\ (-1)^{2j_\ell},&\ell=6-k-r\quad(k\ne r). \end{cases}\]

Thus only the spin of the particle absent from the pair $(k,r)$ matters. An integer spin gives $+1$; a half-integer spin gives $-1$.

Chain $k$ / reference $r$123
1$1$$(-1)^{2j_3}$$(-1)^{2j_2}$
2$(-1)^{2j_3}$$1$$(-1)^{2j_1}$
3$(-1)^{2j_2}$$(-1)^{2j_1}$$1$

For example, comparing chain $(12)3$ with reference $(23)1$ means $k=3,r=1$: $A_{\rm CD}^{3;1}=(-1)^{2j_2}e^{i\lambda_1\gamma_1}A_{\rm TBD}^{3;1}$. The sign is set by particle 2, while the continuous phase uses particle 1. Neither is determined by the parent spin alone.

Which angles are we comparing?

Work in the parent rest frame, with massive daughters and a nondegenerate event. Momentum conservation puts the three momenta in one plane. The canonical reference event has the isobar along $+z$, spectator $r$ along $-z$, and the cyclic first daughter in the positive-$x$ half-plane. Its normal is $+y$.

The three orientation angles rotate this canonical event into the supplied four-vectors:

\[R(\Omega_r)=R_z(\alpha_r)R_y(\beta_r)R_z(\gamma_r).\]

To obtain them, follow the reference chain: $(\alpha_r,\beta_r)$ orient its isobar in the parent frame; after going into that isobar’s helicity frame, the first daughter’s azimuth is $\gamma_r$. In the compatibility helper this is reference_plane_orientation(three_body_topology(r), objs) and the result is (α, cosβ, γ). Use the same $r$ for this operation and for TBD evaluation. The overall orientation rotates the parent index with $D^{j_0*}(\Omega_r)$.

There are three different kinds of angles here:

AnglesPurpose
$\Omega_r=(\alpha_r,\beta_r,\gamma_r)$Orient the reference event relative to external axes.
$\theta_{ij}$Describe the inner two-body decay in chain $k$.
$\zeta^a_{k(r)}$Align leg $a$ of chain $k$ with reference $r$ in the canonical plane.

TBD’s internal alignments are pure $y$ rotations. Obtain their signed angles from cosζ(wr(k,r,a), σs, ms^2) and ispositive(wr(k,r,a)), not from acos alone. CD instead follows a rotation and boost at each vertex, so comparisons between its local frames generally produce full Euler triples. Those extra Euler angles describe the local axes and their rotation paths; they are not additional independent kinematic variables or a nonplanar decay.

Why the spectator phase remains

Fix the Wigner convention

\[D^j_{mn}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma) =e^{-im\alpha}d^j_{mn}(\beta)e^{-in\gamma}.\]

The reference cascade $0\to R+r$, $R\to i+j$ contains

\[D^{j_0*}_{m_0,\lambda_R-\lambda_r}(\alpha_r,\beta_r,0) D^{j_R*}_{\lambda_R,\lambda_i-\lambda_j}(\gamma_r,\theta_{ij},0).\]

The second factor contributes $e^{+i\lambda_R\gamma_r}$: the plus sign follows from complex conjugation. Moving the inner azimuth into the third Euler angle of the parent factor contributes $e^{+i(\lambda_R-\lambda_r)\gamma_r}$. Their ratio is therefore $e^{+i\lambda_r\gamma_r}$, giving

\[\begin{aligned} &D^{j_0*}_{m_0,\lambda_R-\lambda_r}(\alpha_r,\beta_r,0) D^{j_R*}_{\lambda_R,\lambda_i-\lambda_j}(\gamma_r,\theta_{ij},0)\\ &\quad=D^{j_0*}_{m_0,\lambda_R-\lambda_r}(\Omega_r) e^{i\lambda_r\gamma_r} d^{j_R}_{\lambda_R,\lambda_i-\lambda_j}(\theta_{ij}). \end{aligned}\]

TBD includes the overall $D^*$ but does not include this remaining spectator phase. At fixed reference and external helicities, the phase is common to all chains. Do not substitute the spectator or inner azimuth of each target chain.

Where the minus signs come from

A spatial rotation does not uniquely specify its action on a spinor: a full turn leaves spatial axes unchanged but multiplies spin $j$ by $(-1)^{2j}$. Two distinctions prevent most sign mistakes.

First, a negative planar angle may be written using positive middle Euler angle:

\[R_y(-\theta)=R_z(\pi)R_y(\theta)R_z(-\pi).\]

This identity is exact for spinors and adds no sign. Replacing the last $-\pi$ by $+\pi$, however, adds a full turn:

\[D^j(\pi,\theta,\pi)=(-1)^{2j}d^j(-\theta).\]

Second, TBD uses direct principal signed angles in $[-\pi,\pi]$. The positive cyclic parent angles sum to $2\pi$, so composing two pairwise alignments can differ from the direct third alignment by a full turn. CD’s vertex paths can also reverse and restore local plane normals through azimuthal $\pi$ rotations. Compare the spin matrices, not just their spatial axes or Euler angles modulo $2\pi$.

A way to compute, rather than guess, the residual signs is to compare the frame maps in the spin-$1/2$ representation. Define the diagonal spectator phase $P_a(q)_{mm'}=\delta_{mm'}e^{im\gamma_q\delta_{aq}}$, and let $C_a(k\leftarrow r)$ be CD’s conjugated final-leg Wigner matrix, with rows in the chain frame and columns in the reference frame. With the same index order for TBD’s $d_a(k,r)$, compare the two sides of

\[\begin{aligned} D^{j_0*}(\Omega_k)&=\epsilon_0^{2j_0}D^{j_0*}(\Omega_r)d_0(k,r),\\ P_a(k)C_a(k\leftarrow r)&=\epsilon_a^{2j_a}d_a(k,r)P_a(r). \end{aligned}\]

At spin $1/2$, form $L R^\dagger$ from each pair of matrices before inserting $\epsilon_a$. It must be $+I$ or $-I$, which gives $\epsilon_a$ directly. A noncentral residual is a mismatch of conventions or kinematics, not a sign that can be repaired by choosing $\pm1$.

The full amplitude sign is $\epsilon_0^{2j_0}\prod_a\epsilon_a^{2j_a}$. Allowed angular-momentum couplings obey $2j_0=2j_1+2j_2+2j_3\pmod 2$, so introduce $q_a=\epsilon_0\epsilon_a$:

\[S(k,r)=\prod_{a=1}^3q_a^{2j_a}.\]

The cyclic paths give $q_a=-1$ only for the particle absent from $(k,r)$; for $k=r$ all are $+1$. This gives the sign table above, for any allowed spin magnitudes and LS couplings. Individual $\epsilon_a$ assignments can change with Euler branches; the reduced rule keeps the complete chain sign.

Brute-force check on four-vectors

This notebook checks every complex helicity component against the predicted relation, without fitting a phase or choosing the better of $+A$ and $-A$. It samples interior Dalitz points with independent overall rotations, covers all nine chain/reference pairs and eleven spin assignments (including integer spins, several fermions, and spin $3/2$), and enumerates matched allowed LS couplings for two isobar-spin choices per case. Unit line shapes isolate the angular convention; the masses are those of $\Lambda_c\to pK\pi$, with toy spin assignments.

The implementation lives in hidden executable cells below. Rendering this notebook reruns the assertions and records the results; no separate test script is needed. The check also requires detectable errors when either the continuous phase or the discrete sign is omitted.

52800 tensor comparisons; 11 spin assignments; 100 sampled events; all 9 (k,r) pairs
Maximum absolute component error: 4.041e-14
Omitting either the phase or sign is detected. Seed: 42

The residual is the largest absolute difference across all compared components. Each comparison must satisfy error ≤ atol + rtol * scale, with both tolerances set to 2e-10. This finite numerical stress test supports the general relation; it is not a proof for unbounded spins. Exact Euler poles and degenerate phase-space boundaries are excluded and require a shared limiting-frame prescription.

Using the relation in a coherent model

At fixed reference, convert each CD coefficient using $c_{\mathrm{TBD},n}^{(r)}=S(k_n,r)c_{\mathrm{CD},n}$. The spectator phase is common to all terms at fixed external helicities and cancels from unpolarized intensities. Keep it when comparing amplitudes or spin-density information.

Changing a TBD model’s reference from $r$ to $s$ also requires

\[c_{\mathrm{TBD},n}^{(s)} =S(k_n,s)S(k_n,r)c_{\mathrm{TBD},n}^{(r)}.\]

Transform the external spin bases as well when comparing tensors. Changing only reference_k while keeping numerical coefficients can change interference. Store the reference with the fitted couplings.
